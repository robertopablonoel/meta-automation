# Bubby's Place — Avatar Sheet

**Brand:** Bubby's Place
**Type:** 501(c)(3) Nonprofit — Foster Care Support, Hampton Roads VA
**Goal of ads:** Organic social (Instagram, Facebook) to educate, build emotional connection, and drive donations

---

## Who Sees These Ads: The Donor Avatar

The ad audience is NOT the foster child. It's the community member who has the capacity to give — financially, emotionally, or through their network.

---

### Primary Donor — The Community-Connected Parent

**Demographics:**
- Age: 35–60
- Location: Hampton Roads, VA (Suffolk, Virginia Beach, Norfolk, Chesapeake, Portsmouth, Hampton, Newport News)
- HHI: $65K–$150K
- Likely has children of their own
- May be involved in church, Rotary, PTA, or local civic organizations
- Homeowner. Established in the community.

**Psychographics:**
- Values: family, faith, giving back, community roots
- Self-identity: "good neighbor," "community member," person who helps
- Already charitable — gives to church, food bank, community events
- Does NOT identify as an activist or political donor
- Skeptical of large national nonprofits ("where does the money actually go?")
- Responds to *local*, *specific*, and *tangible*

**What they know about foster care:**
- They know it exists. They don't know the gaps.
- They assume "the system" covers kids' basic needs. It doesn't.
- Learning that foster children arrive with *nothing* — no car seat, no diapers, no school supplies — is genuinely shocking to them.
- The education goal is the bridge: once they know the gap exists, the ask makes emotional sense.

**What moves them to donate:**
- A specific, concrete gap (not general "helping kids")
- A local tie — this is *our community*, these are *our kids*
- A real story or face (not statistics)
- The sense that their donation does something traceable: "$30 buys a crib" not "your gift makes an impact"
- Knowing the founders have personal skin in the game (Tom & Wendy were foster parents themselves)

**What blocks them:**
- "I don't know if this is legit"
- "My donation is too small to matter"
- "I'm not a foster parent — this isn't really my problem"
- "I already give to enough causes"

**Emotional state when scrolling:**
- Not in crisis. Not actively seeking a cause. Scrolling casually.
- Must be interrupted by something that feels *real*, not produced.
- Authentic > polished. Community > corporate.

**Language they use:**
- "I had no idea..."
- "That breaks my heart."
- "I wish I could do more."
- "Where do I donate?"
- "Can I share this?"

---

### Secondary Donor — The Connected Young Adult (25–35)

- College-educated, early career
- Shares causes on social media; influenced by community presence
- May have a personal connection to foster care or know someone who does
- Moves through: emotional hook → shares post → eventually donates small amounts
- Most valuable as a **network amplifier** first, donor second

---

### Tertiary Audience — Foster Parent / Caregiver

- Already in the foster system
- Seeing an ad about Bubby's Place tells them *help exists*
- They are a beneficiary, not a donor — but they share content and become social proof
- Their testimonials and stories are the creative fuel for donor-facing ads

---

## Who the Ads Are About: The Beneficiary Profiles

These are the people whose stories power the creative. The donors see their own story through the beneficiary — the child they wish they could have helped.

---

### Profile 1 — The Newborn in Crisis

A baby placed in foster care on 24-hour notice. The foster family gets a call: a newborn is available tonight. They say yes. They have *nothing*. No crib. No diapers. No formula. The system provides placement; it doesn't provide the crib.

**The image this creates:** A foster parent holding a newborn at 2am, texting for help, scrambling to find basics.

**The emotion:** Urgency, vulnerability, the immediate reality of the gap.

**The hook:** "When a baby arrives tonight, they don't come with a crib."

---

### Profile 2 — The School-Age Kid Who Can't Fit In

A 9-year-old in foster care. Three schools in two years. New school, no backpack, shoes that don't fit. Other kids notice. The teacher notices. Foster parents are doing their best but the state stipend doesn't cover everything.

**The image this creates:** A child standing outside a school, backpack-less. Or a small hand holding a new backpack for the first time.

**The emotion:** The slow ache of a kid who just wants to belong.

**The hook:** "She'd been in 3 schools in 2 years. All she wanted was to fit in."

---

### Profile 3 — The 18-Year-Old Who Ages Out Alone

At 18, foster care ends. The system closes the file. No family safety net. No cosigner for an apartment. No one to call. Bubby's Place provides scholarships for community college and trade school — the bridge between foster care and a real future.

**The image this creates:** A young adult in a cap and gown, first in their family (or foster family) to graduate.

**The emotion:** Hope, resilience, the power of one opportunity.

**The hook:** "The system stopped at 18. Bubby's Place didn't."

---

### Profile 4 — The Foster Family Stretched Thin

A couple fostering two kids while working full time. They said yes to fostering because they wanted to help. They didn't expect how much help *they'd* need. Bubby's Place fills the gap — not with judgment, but with diapers, car seats, and a community that shows up.

**The emotion:** Relief. Being seen. Community showing up.

**The hook:** "They said yes to fostering. We said yes to them."

---

## National Foster Care Month (May) Context

Tom Powell's specific campaign goal: use May (National Foster Care Month) to **educate** the community about what foster families actually need, and drive them to **donate**. The campaign comp is **Friends of Foster Care** — look up their social presence as a benchmark.

May is the single biggest awareness moment in the foster care calendar. Messaging should lean into education ("most people don't know...") rather than pure emotional ask.

## Key Facts for Copy

- **Bubby's Place is named after a baby** — Tom & Wendy Powell fostered a newborn they called "Bubby." The name carries warmth and specificity.
- **Founders have skin in the game** — they were foster parents themselves. This isn't a cause they support from a distance.
- **Board member Patrick Ryan aged out of foster care** — lived experience on the board.
- **S. Chris Jones adopted a daughter from foster care** — personal stakes at the leadership level.
- **Geographic anchor:** Suffolk, VA + all of Hampton Roads. Local = trustworthy.
- **501(c)(3) = tax-deductible** — mention in CTAs where appropriate.
- **No single physical location** — this is a community organization, not a building. That's a feature: they go where the need is.
- **Foster Family Fun Fest:** June 6, Windsor Castle Park, Smithfield VA, free for foster families — a shareable event for community reach.
