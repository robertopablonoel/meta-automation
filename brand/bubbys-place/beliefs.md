# Bubby's Place — Necessary Beliefs

**Framework:** Agora E5 — six beliefs a person must hold before they will donate or take meaningful action.

These are the beliefs the ad creative must install or reinforce. Each belief is a prerequisite for the next. If someone skips one, the ask falls flat.

---

## Belief 1 — Foster children in our community have real, urgent needs that aren't being met.

**What they currently think:** "The system takes care of foster kids. That's what it's for."

**What they need to believe:** The system covers placement — it doesn't cover the crib, the diapers, the car seat, the backpack, the sports registration. Foster families absorb those costs personally, or children go without.

**The gap is specific and local, not abstract and national.**

**How to install this belief:**
- Lead with a specific scenario: "When a newborn is placed with a foster family on 24-hour notice, they don't arrive with a crib."
- Use numbers to make it concrete: "The monthly state stipend is $400–$600. A crib costs $150. That's before diapers, formula, or wipes."
- Name the community: Hampton Roads. Suffolk. Virginia Beach. Not "somewhere out there."

**Copy angle:** "Most people assume the system covers it. It doesn't."

---

## Belief 2 — This is a local, real organization — not a distant charity bureaucracy.

**What they currently think:** "I don't know this organization. I don't know if it's legit. I don't know where my money actually goes."

**What they need to believe:** Bubby's Place was started by foster parents who did it themselves. The founders have personal stakes — Tom & Wendy Powell fostered a newborn. Board member Patrick Ryan aged out of the system. S. Chris Jones adopted his daughter from foster care. This isn't charity-from-a-distance. This is people who know.

**The organization serves the same cities, schools, and neighborhoods you know.**

**How to install this belief:**
- Name the founders and their personal story (the baby they called "Bubby")
- Name the board members and their lived connection to foster care
- Name the geographic areas served — recognizable local landmarks, not generic "Hampton Roads"
- Cite community partners: North Suffolk Rotary, VFK CASA (known local entities)
- Note: 501(c)(3) status confirmed — donations are tax-deductible

**Copy angle:** "We weren't planning to start a nonprofit. We were planning to foster a baby."

---

## Belief 3 — A small donation from me creates a specific, traceable impact.

**What they currently think:** "My $25 is a drop in the bucket. It won't make a difference. It goes somewhere vague."

**What they need to believe:** Their donation does something specific and immediate. $15 = backpack and supplies. $30 = a crib for an emergency placement. $50 = diapers for a month. These are not abstractions — they're the actual items Bubby's Place provides to foster families.

**How to install this belief:**
- Anchor to concrete items: "Your donation buys ___" not "your gift makes a difference"
- Tell one story of one family and how the specific item changed something
- Show the scale of the problem and the scale of the solution in the same frame: "One in four foster families doesn't have the supplies they need for a new placement. Your crib changes that for one baby."

**Copy angle:** "$30 buys the crib. No foster parent should start from zero."

---

## Belief 4 — I can help without becoming a foster parent.

**What they currently think:** "I'm not in a position to foster. That's the only way to actually help."

**What they need to believe:** The most important thing a community member can do isn't necessarily fostering — it's showing up for foster families and children in ways that work for their own life. Donating, sharing, attending the Foster Family Fun Fest, volunteering. These acts form the community infrastructure that makes fostering sustainable.

**The point:** Fostering is a calling. But fostering only works if the community supports it. You are part of that.

**How to install this belief:**
- "You don't have to foster to help."
- "The families who say yes need a community that says yes with them."
- Feature non-foster donors and volunteers as the heroes of specific stories
- Mention multiple action tiers: donate, share, volunteer, attend events

**Copy angle:** "They said yes to fostering. We said yes to them. You can too."

---

## Belief 5 — My community has a responsibility to these children — and capacity to act.

**What they currently think:** "This is a big systemic problem. My involvement won't move the needle."

**What they need to believe:** Foster care is a community problem with a community solution. When local people show up — with cribs, with backpacks, with donations, with attendance at events — children in their own ZIP codes have better outcomes. This isn't about fixing the system. It's about filling the gap the system left in their backyard.

**How to install this belief:**
- "These are our kids. In our schools. On our streets."
- "When a community comes together, small acts of generosity create a powerful ripple." (verbatim from Bubby's Place — use it)
- Cite local partners acting locally: Dr. Chase Bollig giving chiropractic care, North Suffolk Rotary showing up — individual community members making it real
- Make May (National Foster Care Month) a moment of community identity: "This is the month Hampton Roads shows up for its foster families."

**Copy angle:** "This is a Hampton Roads problem. Let's make it a Hampton Roads solution."

---

## Belief 6 — This moment (May, National Foster Care Month) is the right time to act.

**What they currently think:** "I'll do something about this later."

**What they need to believe:** May is National Foster Care Month — the one moment a year the entire country pays attention to foster care. A donation or share right now amplifies during the period of maximum visibility. Acting now means your action travels further.

**Urgency note:** Unlike a product with a sale deadline, nonprofit urgency must be earned — not fabricated. Don't manufacture scarcity. Instead:
- Frame May as a *meaningful* moment, not a gimmick
- "This month, we're raising awareness about what foster families actually need — and your support travels farther during this window"
- Foster Family Fun Fest (June 6) creates a hard-date event hook

**How to install this belief:**
- "National Foster Care Month. One month to change what people know."
- "This May, Hampton Roads is talking about the gap. Your share adds to that."
- "Foster Family Fun Fest, June 6 — register now. Free for foster families."
- Create a sense that the conversation is already happening and they can join it, not miss it

**Copy angle:** "It's National Foster Care Month. This is the moment people are listening. Say something."

---

## Belief Sequence in Ads

Most organic social ads will only install 1–2 beliefs at a time. A single post cannot do all six. Sequence them:

| Ad type | Primary belief(s) |
|---|---|
| Education post (gap awareness) | Belief 1 |
| Founder story / origin | Belief 2 |
| Specific item donation ask | Belief 3 |
| "You can help without fostering" | Belief 4 |
| Community identity / event | Belief 5 |
| May campaign / time-sensitive ask | Belief 6 |

The goal of the organic calendar is to cycle through all six beliefs over the course of May, building trust before asking for money.
