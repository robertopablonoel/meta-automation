# Bubby's Place — Brand Instructions

## BRAND IDENTITY
Bubby's Place, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit foster care support organization serving Hampton Roads, VA. Content is organic Facebook and Instagram posts targeting community members who can donate, share, or attend events — not the foster children themselves.

## COPY RULES
**CRITICAL OVERRIDE — READ FIRST:** This is organic Instagram/Facebook content, NOT paid direct-response advertising. Disregard any pipeline instruction to write in "DR style" or "direct-response ad copy" format — those apply to paid ad brands. This brand's content is organic social posts: community-rooted captions that educate and connect, not sales copy that converts cold traffic.

- **Primary Text**: 3–5 sentences. This is the Instagram/Facebook caption — write it as a complete, self-contained post. Structure: (1) Hook — a specific, surprising local gap the reader didn't know. Must work standalone as the first sentence because Instagram truncates at ~125 characters. Never open with setup or context-building. (2) Human moment — one child, one family, one named object in a specific scene. (3) Single concrete action — donate with a price anchor ($30 = a crib), share, or register for an event. Append 4–6 hashtags at the end: always include #FosterCare #HamptonRoads #BubbysPlace, then 1–3 from #SuffolkVA #NationalFosterCareMonth #FosterCareAwareness #CommunityFirst #HamptonRoadsGives based on angle.
- **Headline**: Under 40 characters. The sharpest, most specific line from the post — a surprising local fact, a hard truth, or a direct question. Examples: "The crib doesn't come with the baby.", "The system stops at 18.", "These are our kids.", "No crib. No warning.", "$30 changes tonight." Do NOT write vague charity slogans or generic taglines.
- **Description**: Under 30 characters. One concrete CTA. Examples: "Donate at bubbysplace.org", "$30 buys a crib.", "Register — June 6", "Share this post.", "bubbysplace.org". One action. Specific.

## COPY STYLE GUIDELINES
- Sound like a community member writing a local newsletter — not a nonprofit running a campaign. Warm, honest, specific. Short sentences. Real facts. Zero marketing language.
- Lead with what the reader doesn't know. "Most people assume the system covers it. It doesn't." is the single most powerful opener for this brand — use it or a variant whenever installing Belief 1.
- Anchor every ask to a named item and price: "$30 = a crib" not "your gift makes a difference" not "donate today"
- Name the geography every time: Hampton Roads, Suffolk VA, Virginia Beach, Smithfield — never "our community" or "the area"
- Use real names when they fit: Tom & Wendy Powell (founders, former foster parents), Patrick Ryan (board member who aged out of foster care at 18), S. Chris Jones (adopted his daughter from foster care)
- When any of these fit naturally, use them verbatim: "We weren't planning to start a nonprofit. We were planning to foster a baby." / "The system stopped at 18. Bubby's Place didn't." / "When a community comes together, small acts of generosity create a powerful ripple."
- Pair every stat with a human moment — a child's name, a specific object, a recognizable scene. Stats without a person are inert.
- Never write: "transforming lives," "making a difference," "empowering communities," "dedicated to helping," "together we can," "your generous support," "impact," or any phrase from a generic charity mailer.
- No guilt. No "children are suffering while you scroll." Show the child after the gap was filled — not in it.
- Do NOT use Schwartz market sophistication stage language in copy. Use the Agora E5 belief framework from beliefs.md. Each variation should install one or two beliefs — vary which belief across the set (Belief 1: gap awareness, Belief 2: founder credibility, Belief 3: concrete impact, Belief 4: anyone can help, Belief 5: community identity, Belief 6: May urgency).
