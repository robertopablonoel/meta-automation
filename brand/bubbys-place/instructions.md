# Bubby's Place — Brand Instructions

## BRAND IDENTITY
Bubby's Place, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit foster care support organization serving Hampton Roads, VA. Content is organic Facebook and Instagram posts targeting community members who can donate, share, or attend events — not the foster children themselves.

## BRAND DOCS
avatar.md
mission-brief.md
beliefs.md

## COPY RULES
- **Primary Text**: 3-5 sentences. Education-first. Lead with a specific, surprising local gap that reframes what they thought they knew. Follow with a human moment (one child, one family, one named object). End with a single concrete action — donate amount + item, share, or event. Copy is the caption of the organic post.
- **Headline**: Under 40 characters. A specific stat, gap statement, or community-rooted hook. Examples: "The crib doesn't come with the baby.", "The system stops at 18.", "These are our kids.", "Hampton Roads shows up.", "They said yes. We did too." Do NOT write vague charity slogans.
- **Description**: 40-80 characters. CTA details or event info. Combine 2-3 of: donation URL (bubbysplace.org), dollar anchor + item, 501(c)(3) tax-deductible note, event details (Foster Family Fun Fest, June 6). Examples: "Donate at bubbysplace.org. Tax-deductible.", "$30 buys a crib. bubbysplace.org", "Foster Family Fun Fest — June 6. Free for foster families.", "Share this. Someone you know doesn't know yet."

## COPY STYLE GUIDELINES
- Tone: warm, honest, community-rooted, hopeful without being saccharine. Short sentences. Specific facts. Sound like a local newsletter, not a PSA.
- Always name the geography: Hampton Roads, Suffolk VA, Virginia Beach — not "our community" in the abstract
- Use concrete dollar anchors tied to named items: "$30 = a crib" not "your gift makes a difference"
- Use real names when available: Tom & Wendy Powell, Patrick Ryan (board member who aged out of the system)
- Pair every stat with a human moment — stats without a person behind them are inert
- End with a clear, single action: donate / share / register / follow
- NEVER use: poverty porn, guilt-heavy messaging, generic nonprofit language ("empowering communities," "making a difference"), national-scale language, manufactured urgency
- Verbatim brand lines (use directly when appropriate): "Meeting needs and changing lives in foster care.", "When a community comes together, small acts of generosity create a powerful ripple.", "We weren't planning to start a nonprofit. We were planning to foster a baby."
- Before writing any variation, note which Belief (1–6) from the beliefs doc this post is installing — keep the May calendar sequenced across all six beliefs
- Do NOT use Schwartz market sophistication stages — use the Agora E5 belief framework from the brand context instead
