# Bubby's Place — Mission Brief

**Brand:** Bubby's Place
**Type:** 501(c)(3) Nonprofit — Hampton Roads, VA
**Primary ask (the "offer"):** Donate. Share. Show up.
**Campaign context:** National Foster Care Month (May 2026) — educate and drive donations

---

## What Bubby's Place Is

A community-funded nonprofit that fills the material and financial gaps the foster care system leaves behind. Founded by Tom & Wendy Powell — former foster parents — after they experienced firsthand how little the system actually provides.

The name comes from a baby they fostered. They called him "Bubby." That relationship continues. That's the level of personal investment behind this organization.

**One-line mission:** "Meeting needs and changing lives in foster care."

**What makes it real:** The founders didn't start this from a distance. They were in a hospital room with a newborn and no crib. They became foster parents. They stayed connected. This is lived, not theorized.

---

## The Gap (The Problem We Solve)

Most people assume the foster care system provides for children's basic needs. It doesn't — not fully.

**What the system provides:** A monthly stipend (often $400–$600/mo in Virginia) and placement coordination.

**What the system doesn't cover:**
- Diapers, wipes, formula for infants
- Cribs, car seats for emergency placements
- School supplies, backpacks, shoes
- Club fees, sports registration, field trips
- College tuition or trade school tools for youth aging out

Foster parents absorb these costs personally — or children go without.

**The hardest truth:** At 18, foster care ends. The state file closes. If no family has adopted them, they leave the system with no support structure. Bubby's Place provides scholarships for community college and trade school — often the only institutional support they get.

---

## The Three Programs

### Caretakers (Infant & Toddler Support)
Support for foster parents receiving emergency placements — the newborn who arrives with nothing. Diapers, wipes, cribs, car seats, infant clothing.

**Message:** "When a baby arrives on 24-hour notice, they don't come with supplies. We make sure no foster parent starts from zero."

### Students (School-Age Assistance)
Backpacks, school supplies, shoes, clothing, club fees, sports registration. Removing the visible markers that separate foster kids from their peers.

**Message:** "Every child deserves to show up ready. Not just for school — for the chance to belong."

### Careers (Transition to Independence)
Scholarships for community college and trade school funding for youth aging out at 18. The bridge when the system closes the door.

**Message:** "The system stopped at 18. Bubby's Place didn't."

## The "Offer" — What We're Asking For

Unlike a DTC product, there's no transaction with a clear deliverable. The conversion funnel is:

```
AWARENESS — "I didn't know foster families needed this"
    ↓
EDUCATION — "The system doesn't cover this. Real kids are affected."
    ↓
EMOTIONAL CONNECTION — "I want to help this specific kid / family"
    ↓
CONVERSION — Donate | Share | Volunteer | Attend event
```

**Primary CTA:** Donate at bubbysplace.org
**Secondary CTAs:** Share this post | Follow for more | Register for Foster Family Fun Fest

**Donation framing (make it concrete):**
- "$15 = backpack and school supplies"
- "$30 = a crib for an emergency placement"
- "$50 = a month of diapers for a foster infant"
- "$200 = one month of trade school supplies for an aging-out youth"
- "Any amount, donated monthly, means a family never goes without"

These price anchors are not exact — use directionally and adjust if official donation tiers are provided.

## Messaging Pillars

### 1. Local. Real. Not a charity campaign.
This is your community. These are neighbors, schools, parks you recognize. Not a distant organization with stock photos of generic suffering.

*Tone: community newsletter, not national PSA*

### 2. The gap is surprising. Most people don't know.
Lead with education. "Most people assume..." is a powerful opener. Once they know the gap exists, the ask becomes obvious.

*Tone: eye-opening, not guilt-tripping*

### 3. Concrete, specific impact.
"Your donation buys diapers" beats "your gift makes a difference." The more specific the impact, the more credible the ask.

*Tone: direct, honest, accountable*

### 4. The founders have skin in the game.
This isn't a charity run by people who've never touched the problem. Tom & Wendy were foster parents. Patrick Ryan aged out of the system. Real stakes create real trust.

*Tone: personal, human, not institutional*

### 5. Community as the solution.
Foster care is a systemic problem but individual acts add up. "When a community comes together, small acts of generosity create a powerful ripple" — this is verbatim from their website and reflects the organizational ethos.

*Tone: "we" language, collective action, not guilt-based individual obligation*

## What Bubby's Place Is NOT

- A foster care agency (they don't place children)
- A shelter or group home
- A national organization with a bureaucratic structure
- A cause that requires expertise or fostering to support
- A charity that reports impact in vague terms

**Anti-patterns to avoid in copy:**
- Poverty porn — don't show suffering, show possibility
- Guilt-heavy messaging — "children are suffering while you scroll" approach erodes trust
- Generic nonprofit language ("empowering communities," "making a difference")
- National scale language — this is Hampton Roads, speak to Hampton Roads

## Brand Voice

**Tone:** Warm, personal, honest, community-rooted, hopeful without being saccharine.

**Writing style:** Short sentences. Specific facts. Real stories. Less formal than a government nonprofit; more human than a corporate charity.

**What "Bubby's Place" sounds like:**
- "We weren't planning to start a nonprofit. We were planning to foster a baby."
- "She'd been in 3 schools in 2 years. All she wanted was a backpack that was hers."
- "When a community comes together, small acts of generosity create a powerful ripple."
- "Meeting needs and changing lives in foster care."
